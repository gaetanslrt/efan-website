History of FASN (French American School of Norfolk)

The origins (2010-2011)

In 2010, the French Armed Forces General Staff (État-Major des Armées, EMA), together with several contacts at the French Embassy in the United States, noted that there was no French school in the Norfolk area, even though the number of children to be schooled kept growing.

The French military representative in Norfolk, the Élément de soutien national (ESN), then designed a project to create a French American school. He sought the support and expertise of John and Simone Ryan, a French American couple from Hampton Roads without whom the project could never have come to life. John M. Ryan, an attorney, was the founder of the Norfolk Blues Rugby Club and the president of the Maritime Association. Simone Chrétien Ryan, a law librarian by profession, is still today the founder and President of the school’s Board of Directors. The Mission laïque française (MLF) contributed its expertise to the initiative.

The Mayor of Norfolk, who was closely involved in the project, agreed that the French school should be hosted on the premises of American schools. The Norfolk Public Schools (NPS) Board of Education and the principals of the American schools concerned worked closely with the ESN of Norfolk and the MLF to build a French American educational partnership.

The opening of the school

In September 2011, the French American School of Norfolk (FASN) welcomed its first students at Crossroads Elementary School, Norview Middle School and Granby High School. The elementary and middle schools were later brought together at Crossroads Elementary and Middle School.

Expansion to Virginia Beach

As most military families live in Virginia Beach, a new partnership was signed in 2022 with Virginia Beach City Public Schools (VBCPS). FASN thus gradually opened new classes in Virginia Beach schools: Cox High School in September 2022, Great Neck Middle School in September 2023 and John B. Dey Elementary School in September 2024. The FASN program at Granby High School, in Norfolk, closed in June 2025.

A school supported by its partners

FASN exists thanks to the commitment of the French Armed Forces General Staff, and to the support of the Mission laïque française (MLF) and of the French Embassy and Consulate in Washington D.C. It also owes its existence to the commitment of the authorities and schools of Norfolk and Virginia Beach, which host the school and its students free of charge.
