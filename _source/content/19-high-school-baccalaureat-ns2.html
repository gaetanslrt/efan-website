Preparing for the diploma

FASN teachers, who follow the curriculum published in the Ministry of National Education’s Bulletin officiel (BO), together with CNED materials, prepare 11th and 12th grade (première and terminale) students for the baccalauréat. In the subjects taught at FASN, students sit written and oral mock exams under the same conditions as in France. In 12th grade, the teachers of the specialty subjects concerned help students prepare for the Grand oral.

Registration

FASN holds an information meeting to explain how to register for the exams on the Cyclades platform.

Independent candidate status (candidat libre or candidat individuel)

FASN students are registered for the baccalauréat as independent candidates. Only the exams taken at the end of the school year count towards the diploma: continuous assessment is not taken into account. These exams take place at the Lycée Rochambeau, in Bethesda, on a calendar that differs from that of France.

At present, the Lycée Rochambeau does not hold exams for certain specialty subjects. Students must therefore choose specialty subjects offered by the Lycée Rochambeau. Otherwise, the exams will have to be taken in France. Families should check with the exam coordinator.

How the exams work

The way the exams are taken differs from France (see the comparison tables below).

In France, continuous assessment grades count towards the baccalauréat in some subjects. This is not the case for independent candidates: the grades earned during the year at FASN are not taken into account. For each subject concerned, continuous assessment is replaced by an exam at the end of the year, called an “épreuve ponctuelle”, held at Rochambeau. For these subjects, an independent candidate’s baccalauréat is similar to the one previous generations knew: the student is assessed once, at the end of the year.

Is taking the baccalauréat as an independent candidate a disadvantage?

The format is similar to the one today’s parents knew: one exam at the end of the year for each subject. Moreover, some French higher education institutions view continuous assessment grades with caution and favor grades from national exams.

For example, in December 2024 the leadership of Sciences Po Paris announced a reform of its entrance exam for 2025 to make up for the limits of the baccalauréat. According to its director, Luis Vassy, continuous assessment results are “overrated” by schools. In addition, moving the written exams to June deprives higher education institutions of “a national written assessment to select candidates”, which they regret. Likewise, some preparatory classes for the grandes écoles (CPGE) and some schools ask applicants to sit entrance exams.

A key choice when registering for the exams

When registering for the exams on the Cyclades platform, students must choose between two options. This choice is decisive, so families should make it carefully:

  • Choice 1: students take the exams on the 11th grade (première) curriculum at the end of 11th grade, then those on the 12th grade (terminale) curriculum at the end of 12th grade. This is the option families choose, because students are only examined on the 11th grade curriculum in the 11th grade exams. In addition, the grades obtained are certified grades, which matters for the Parcoursup application;
  • Choice 2: students take all the exams on the 11th and 12th grade curricula at the end of 12th grade, except French.
