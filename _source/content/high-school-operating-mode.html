How does the high school work?

FASN welcomes students from 9th grade (troisième) to 12th grade (terminale). Classes run from 7:20 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. at the latest. In the morning, students follow the American timetable until 12:35 p.m. American classes are divided into four 90-minute periods called blocks, the fourth being devoted to the first class of the FASN French curriculum. In the afternoon, the French curriculum timetable varies by grade and, for 11th and 12th grade (première and terminale), by the specialty subjects chosen.

American curriculum

American high school normally lasts four years and ends with the high school diploma. Throughout high school, students take required courses and choose electives. They must pass them to earn 22 credits (standard diploma) or 26 credits (advanced diploma). The American timetable takes place in the morning.

When they start at the American high school, students are advised to tell their counselor that they will only be there for a limited time, and to explore with the counselor whether they can earn their high school diploma during that period. The diploma can be earned in less than four years: students submit their French report cards, whose grades are converted into credits.

Language support. On arrival, and until their level of English is deemed sufficient, students are followed by a teacher who specializes in teaching English to non-English-speaking children (English as a Second Language program, ESL). This teacher helps them settle into the school and learn the language.

French curriculum

Students are taught in the FASN French class. Classes are small. In the subjects taught at FASN, teachers follow the curriculum published in the Ministry of National Education’s Bulletin officiel (BO); the other subjects are studied with CNED materials, which ensures continuity in the acquisition of the knowledge, methods and skills specific to the French education system.

Some subjects are studied in class with FASN teachers. The others are studied at home, with students organizing this independent work flexibly, according to their plans and abilities.

Each family freely decides which CNED subjects not taught at FASN their child will take. The teaching team advises families and students on the subjects to study independently, as it has an overall view of their American and French subjects and of their academic and post-baccalauréat plans. The dual curriculum also makes it possible to replace some subjects with their American equivalent: a student can, for example, take Spanish in the American curriculum rather than as LVA or LVB with the CNED.

For the 2026-2027 school year, FASN offers four specialty subjects (mathematics, physics-chemistry, life and earth sciences (SVT) and HGGSP), complemented by the advanced mathematics option (mathématiques expertes). Students must choose specialty subjects offered by the Lycée Rochambeau, where the exams take place; otherwise, the exams will have to be taken in France.

Advantages of this approach

  • avoiding any break with the French school system and making the return to French schools easier;
  • keeping the work habits acquired in French classes, as well as a strong command of French;
  • avoiding working alone at home, in favor of a group and a reassuring setting;
  • benefiting from extensive immersion in the American system;
  • settling into the American system more gradually than students enrolled only in that system, thanks to a group and French reference points;
  • lightening the load on parents and children, since the French classes are taught by FASN, with no correspondence courses;
  • developing students’ adaptability, as they gradually learn to move between two systems with different educational, social and linguistic codes;
  • taking part in educational projects that students could not join on their own, or in an American school alone;
  • building ties with American and French families.

Status of the high school. The Mission laïque française (MLF) nevertheless guarantees that the teaching complies with French standards.

Teachers of the French curriculum. FASN’s French teachers come from the French Ministry of National Education or from higher education. They work at FASN either on secondment or as locally recruited staff, according to their field of expertise and level of education.
