FASN students can apply through Parcoursup and follow the same calendar as candidates schooled in France.

The information taken into account (grades, teachers’ comments, etc.) differs, however, from what is used in France, because the student registers on Parcoursup as a “candidate schooled abroad” (candidat scolarisé à l’étranger).

FASN holds an information meeting to explain the Parcoursup registration process and to advise students on building the strongest possible application. This individual support continues throughout the school year, from the moment the student starts high school. The teaching team advises students on what to include in their application (volunteering, extracurricular activities, etc.) and can write letters of recommendation and support. FASN students are not affected by Parcoursup’s geographic zoning. FASN also helps students apply to American universities through the Common App platform.

Making the most of the dual curriculum

The ability to follow a dual curriculum is a strength to highlight in the Parcoursup application. It is evidenced by the FASN report cards.

FASN report cards are issued every term. They include the grades earned in both the French and the American curricula, the French teachers’ comments and the opinion of the FASN Director. The third-term report card also states the decision on moving up to the next grade and the opinion on specialty subjects for entry into 11th grade (première). These report cards can be certified by the academic inspector of the French Embassy in Washington D.C.
