American curriculum

FASN students take the same assessments as American students, with results expressed as letter grades. As long as their level of English is deemed insufficient, support measures are put in place during assessments.

During their first year as ESL students, their American results are expressed as “Pass” or “Fail”. This way of grading eases their adjustment by limiting the pressure of grades.

Assessments are done on paper or digitally. Results can be viewed on the ParentVUE platform, and an American report card is issued at the end of each grading period.

French curriculum

For the subjects taught at FASN, assessments are designed by the FASN teachers. At their discretion, they may add CNED assessments, marked either by the FASN teachers or by CNED teachers, who belong to the French Ministry of National Education. These assessments take place in class. This arrangement, including for CNED assessments, is a key element in giving weight to the student’s report card with a view to returning to France.

For the CNED subjects not taught at FASN, assessments are those set by the CNED. They are done at home: families send the papers to the CNED for marking themselves.

At the end of each term, a report card is sent to families. It shows the results obtained in both the French and the American curricula, the French teachers’ comments and the opinion of the FASN Director. At the end of the school year, families leaving the United States may request a certification of the report cards, issued by the French Embassy. These documents make the return to France easier.
